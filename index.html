What aids decision making while buying supplements online?

A methodical user-research and conversion optimization program designed to diagnose trust hurdles, buying friction, and pricing models for GNC India.

01 / User Interviews

Unbiased Inquiries

To uncover raw behaviors rather than opinion-gathering on design assets, our conversational questionnaire avoided direct UI discussion completely.

  1. What's your lifestyle like?
  2. How did you hear about GNC?
  3. What makes you trust a nutrition brand?
  4. How do you decide which supplement to take?
  5. Do you wait for offers to buy, or buy as and when needed?
  6. Would you subscribe for a discount?
02 / Field Discoveries

Discovery

  • Instagram dominant via influencer custom discount codes.
  • Word of mouth equally strong: peer friends, gym trainers, and colleagues.
  • Active WhatsApp groups push timely deals and coupon codes.
  • YouTube is highly utilized by research-led analytical buyers.
  • Offline pharmacy chains (Apollo) popular for in-person instant gratification.

Trust

  • Trainer or peer recommendation is the absolute strongest credibility signal.
  • '90 Years' brand heritage anchors older and research-led segments.
  • Functional trust is proven post-purchase by taste, mixability, and lack of bloating.
  • Strict research-led buyers heavily scrutinize ingredient profiles and lab tests.
  • Widespread offline local store availability legitimizes the brand's premium stature.

Purchase Triggers

  • A large price-conscious segment actively waits for deals, believing an offer is always live.
  • Higher-AOV buyers ignore discounts and buy immediately on personal or health need.

Subscription

  • Higher-AOV health planners are highly open to friction-free automated delivery.
  • Price-sensitive cohorts resist subscriptions, wanting to retain flexibility for bigger flash offers.

Confusion

  • Wellness/lifestyle buyers suffer from significantly more confusion than dedicated protein buyers.
  • Back of pack ingredient label is the primary source of personal reference.
  • Coached/guided fitness enthusiasts experience the least decision paralysis.

Buying Patterns

  • Single-product checkout is dominant; basket bundling is not instinctively natural.
  • Instinctive pairings do exist: Protein + Creatine, or Protein + Multivitamins.
  • Restock habits are strictly monthly, mapping directly to standardized packaging volume sizes.

Price Perception

  • GNC is viewed as cost-effective only when an offer is clearly active.
  • The standard no-offer state actively triggers a 'wrong time to buy' anxiety.
  • Budget-constrained consumers plan routines around deal calendars rather than need cycles.
03 / UX Interventions
Intervention 01

Make the site good at confirming

  • Persistent '90 Years Heritage' legacy badge directly on the product detail page.
  • Direct lab test certificate results available via a prominent expandable widget.
  • Clear back-of-package nutritional panel previews embedded inside listing images.
  • Highlighted flavor profiles and mixability feedback scores clearly visible.
  • Official 'Trainer Recommended' contextual tags on high-performing SKUs.
Intervention 02

Give the uncoached buyer a framework

  • Goal-based recommendation quiz based on the customized FAE framework (Goal → Fitness Level → Dietary Preference → Budget → Sensitivities).
  • Expertly curated pre-configured product bundles positioned explicitly as 'trainer recommended logic'.
Intervention 03

Convert the wait into a cycle

  • Direct currency % discount representations explicitly featured on product listings.
  • Automated CRM restock nudges systematically sent at consumer consumption days 22-25.
  • Highly visual wishlist price-drop notifications triggered in real-time.
  • Customizable 'Subscribe & Save' programs displaying explicit absolute rupee savings upfront.

How do you buy a perfume you can’t smell?

01 / User Research & In-Store

User Research

User research with Bellavita buyers on discovery, trust, pricing and gifting behaviour. We wanted to locate exactly how digital and physical signals guide a choice.

In-Store Observations

In-store conversations and observation, including how store staff sell, handle hesitation, translate notes visually, and resolve price objections.

02 / Field Discoveries

Pain Points

  • “Not long lasting” is the single most common complaint — and long-lasting is also the main reason people buy. It directly hurts repeat rate.
  • A minority tolerate lower longevity because of the price trade-off.

Pricing & Discovery

  • Most purchases fall between ₹500–₹1,200. Users are discount buyers.
  • Users test offline, buy online for the best price. Discovery happens primarily offline — Zara, Westside, mall kiosks.
  • Strong aspiration for premium perfume at an affordable price.
  • Most users have no go-to brand. They are switchers.

Trust & Influence

  • Word of mouth from friends and sisters is the most trusted recommendation source.
  • Users don't understand notes — top, heart, base are hidden under “read more” — so they fall back on reviews.
  • Fraud calls are a live problem. Bellavita educates users about the scam over WhatsApp, but not on the PDP.

Scent Language

  • Users describe scent emotionally and visually: “sky,” “ocean,” “smell of rain,” “aquatic.”
  • Language is gender-coded: “girly,” “manly.”

Risk Mitigation

  • Users buy gift sets or testers rather than committing to a full size.
  • Reputation is at stake — people only recommend perfume they've personally used and liked.
  • Budget for an untested product is around ₹500.

In-Store Buying Cues

  • Visual (bottles and displays) trigger sensory associations.
  • Blue packaging maps directly to aquatic/fresh perceptions.
  • Multi-phase scent development journey is tested live on skin.

What the Staff Actually Do

  • Interpret: knows a blue perfume means aquatic and maps choices instantly.
  • Read non-verbal cues: recommends alternatives based on facial reactions.
  • Educate: explains top, mid, and base notes directly as they settle.
04 / UX Interventions
Intervention 01

Fix the longevity trap

Remove explicit hour claims. Shift listing images, description and review prompts to other sensory attributes — energising, magnetic, bold.

Intervention 02

Visualize the smell

Visuals that explain scent development, show how the notes unfold and what the perfume feels like. Imagery for listing images and A+ content focused on the feel of the perfume.

Intervention 03

Speak the user's language

Fix search to match native user vocabulary. Standardise nomenclature. Create a “Find Your Fragrance” interactive choice quiz.

Intervention 04

Rebuild trust

A structured trust program that turns reviews, safety, and education into visible proof points customers can act on.

05 / What We Built: New PDP

First fold

  1. Benefit, not hours“Confidence that stays with you all day” carries longevity as a feeling. No hour count, so no promise the bottle can fail against.
  2. Reviews before price4.9 and 513 reviews sit above the number — the fallback buyers reach for when they can't read notes.
  3. Never a no-offer stateMRP, slashed price and percentage saved together. A card with no visible offer reads as the wrong time to buy.
  4. Best price, made explicitThe ₹549 best-offer price and a persistent “Saving ₹300” bar answer the value-max cue from the store floor.

The Scent Journey

  1. The journey, made navigableTop, heart and base become three steps instead of three words buried under “read more.”
  2. Plain language first“Top notes are the first notes you'll smell.” The education the store staff give, written into the page.
  3. Ingredient → feelingEach note gets a photograph and three feeling-words. Lavender reads calm, timeless, elegant. That's the interpreter's job as a component.
  4. Occasion, not genderSix occasions replace the girly/manly shorthand buyers use when nothing better is offered.

Better Together Combos

  1. Paired by scent familyBoth items carry “Musky,” so the bundle reads as a scent decision rather than a discount coincidence.
  2. Still inside the band₹998 for two keeps a doubled basket inside the ₹500–₹1,200 window, with the saving shown as a percentage and in rupees.
  3. One tapAdd Combo commits the pair without asking a switcher to build it themselves.

06 / What It Became

A unified template system.

The PDP introduced new fonts and colours, which became Bellavita's design system. That system is now the base for the redesign of the remaining pages, and the PDP itself is the base template for skincare and cosmetics.
